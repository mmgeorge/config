# RFC and Proposal Profile

*Read when proposing a change, evaluating options, requesting consensus, or deciding whether to adopt a technology or architecture.*

## Controlling Claim

Establish that one proposal best satisfies the stated goals and constraints after accounting for credible alternatives, drawbacks, risks, and unresolved acceptance conditions.

## Reasoning Obligations

- Define the problem through affected users and concrete use cases.
- State goals, non-goals, constraints, and evaluation criteria that influence selection.
- Present the proposal through examples before exhaustive mechanics when readers need both views.
- Connect evidence to the proposal through explicit warrants.
- Compare credible alternatives at the same level of detail under shared criteria.
- Record drawbacks, qualifications, risks, mitigations, and the impact of doing nothing.
- Identify unresolved questions that affect acceptance.
- Define how implementation or a prototype will validate uncertain claims.

## Default Progression

Move from motivation and criteria through the proposal, concrete examples, reference-level mechanics, alternatives, tradeoffs, risks, validation, and acceptance-relevant open questions.

Use headings fitted to the proposal. Dedicated `Alternatives` or `Risks` sections help when those discussions carry enough weight, but the reasoning matters more than the labels.

## Completion Check

A reviewer should be able to identify the decision being requested, test it against explicit criteria, understand its mechanics and costs, compare it fairly with alternatives, and name the evidence still required before acceptance.
